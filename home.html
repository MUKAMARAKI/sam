MindLeaps — disciplined daily practice since 2014

My journey as a dancer began in 2014, when I first encountered the transformative power of movement through MindLeaps. At that time, I was not yet the artist I am today; I was a student searching for meaning, direction, and a way to express what words often failed to capture. Each day I found myself in the studio, standing before teachers who guided me patiently through new steps, techniques, and rhythms. The repetition of daily practice—sometimes exhausting, often challenging—slowly revealed to me that dance was more than physical exercise. It was a language, a discipline, and a way of life.

Those early years were defined by constant discovery. Every movement introduced by my teachers felt like unlocking a new dimension of expression. I began to realize that dance could be delicate yet powerful, structured yet fluid, individual yet collective. The studio became not only a place of training but also a sanctuary where I could transform emotions—joy, pain, uncertainty, hope—into physical motion. Through this process, I learned resilience, patience, and the importance of showing up every single day, no matter how difficult the journey seemed.

As time passed, my devotion to dance deepened, and opportunities began to open. What began as simple exercises in front of a mirror grew into performances that reached audiences far beyond my imagination. I was invited to perform at some of Rwanda’s most prestigious cultural events. The Ubumuntu Arts Festival, with its global platform for humanity and storytelling, challenged me to use my body not only for aesthetics but for social connection and empathy. At the Kwita Izina Ceremony, one of Rwanda’s most internationally recognized celebrations, I found myself performing in front of large and diverse audiences, representing the beauty of dance as a living expression of culture. Each stage, each performance, each applause reinforced that I was no longer just a student—I was becoming a storyteller, a performer, and a voice within the artistic community.

Between 2014 and today, my path has been marked by a series of transformations. Dance became my discipline, my teacher, and my mirror. I performed at year-end showcases, opening ceremonies, and countless other events, each experience layering my artistry with more depth, confidence, and vision. Every rehearsal was an opportunity to polish technique; every performance was a dialogue with the audience; every collaboration with fellow dancers was a chance to learn humility and innovation.

But beyond the physicality, dance has taught me to see the world differently. It has shown me that movement carries memory, history, and identity. It is not bound by language or borders—it is a universal force that speaks directly to the human soul. My choreographic work strives to embody this philosophy. I aim to create pieces that invite audiences into emotional landscapes, where bodies in motion reflect resilience, vulnerability, and the search for connection.

Looking back, I see the young student in 2014, shyly attempting his first steps at MindLeaps, unsure of where the path would lead. And I see the artist I have become—one who has performed at Rwanda’s most iconic stages, who continues to refine his craft, and who carries a vision of using dance as a tool for storytelling, healing, and transformation. The journey has been long, but it is only the beginning.

Today, I remain committed not only to performing but also to growing as a choreographer, mentor, and cultural ambassador. My aspiration is to inspire the next generation of dancers the way I was once inspired: to show them that through consistency, creativity, and courage, movement can open doors to places they never imagined. My story is still unfolding, but one truth has remained constant since 2014—dance is my language, my art, and my way of being in the world.
